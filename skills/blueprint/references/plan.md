# plan.md: where it is going, and in what order

The grand plan: the path from here to the vision, as a sequence of outcomes, the bets behind that order, the risks that would end it, and the evidence that would make the owner stop or change course. It is written last because it draws on everything else, and it is a plan of outcomes, not of tasks.

## Establishes

| Section | Answers | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| **Mission** | The one sentence the whole plan serves over its horizon | It connects `vision.md` to the targets in `business.md` |
| **Phases** | The path as a sequence of outcomes. For each phase: the outcome, the exit criterion that proves it, what it unlocks, and the themes of work it includes | Each exit criterion is observable ("50 paying clinics retained for three months"), not an activity ("launch v1") |
| **Milestones** | The measurable points that matter, tied to targets | Reference `business.md → Targets` rather than restating them. Horizons are consistent with `stack.md → Resources` and the runway |
| **Bets and sequencing** | Why this order: the assumptions it depends on, and what would change it | Each bet is named with the evidence for it and the phase that tests it |
| **Risks** | The few things that would end or badly damage the project | Each has its early signal and the planned response. Generic risk registers are left out |
| **Kill and pivot criteria** | The evidence that would make the owner stop, pause or change direction, decided in advance | Specific and time-bound ("fewer than 10 paying customers after 6 months of the channel test"). The owner has explicitly agreed to them |
| **Not yet** | What is deliberately deferred: segments, features, markets, platforms | Each has the trigger that brings it in |

## From the owner

- What must be true in 3, 6 and 12 months for them to keep going with confidence?
- What is the first moment the project delivers real value to a real user, and how soon can it get there?
- Which assumptions scare them most? What is the cheapest way to test those first?
- What would make them stop? Agreeing this now, while calm, is one of the most valuable things a blueprint does.
- What are they tempted to do early that should wait?

## Research and compute

- Check the plan against resources: can the stated capacity reach each exit criterion in the implied time? If not, adjust phases or scope with the owner rather than hoping.
- Check milestones against the economic model: revenue milestones are reachable given channel, conversion and churn assumptions, and the runway outlasts the phase that must prove revenue.
- For each risk, look for how similar projects failed. The prior-attempts research behind `vision.md` and the `market.md` research are the first sources.

## Belongs / doesn't

| In | Out |
|---|---|
| Outcomes, exit criteria, bets, risks, kill criteria | Task lists, sprints, tickets, estimates |
| The order and why | Status markers ("done", "in progress"), completion dates |
| Deferrals with triggers | Everything not yet built, listed |

Where the project stands against the plan belongs wherever the project tracks progress. The plan is intent and changes only when intent changes.

## Common weaknesses

- Phases defined by features shipped rather than outcomes proven.
- The riskiest assumption tested last.
- No kill criteria, so the project can never fail and therefore never learns.
- A timeline that ignores the owner's stated time and money.
