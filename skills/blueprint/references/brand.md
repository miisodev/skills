# brand.md: how it is recognised and how it sounds

The identity the owner wants the project to have: its name, the position it claims, its personality and voice, and the direction for how it looks. It sets taste and red lines. It deliberately does not build the design system, which a capable designer or model will do better with this document in hand.

## Establishes

| Section | Answers | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | The name, its meaning, how it is written (casing, spacing, marks), what it is never called | Availability checked: trademark conflicts in the target markets and classes, domain, key social handles, app-store name. Results recorded in `research/` |
| **Positioning** | The one idea the brand owns in the buyer's mind | Consistent with `market.md → Positioning`, expressed as the brand claim, and something a competitor could not credibly say |
| **Personality** | 3–5 traits, each with what it means *and* what it does not ("confident, not arrogant") | Traits are distinct from each other and from the category's defaults |
| **Voice** | How it sounds in writing | Each trait has a do line and a don't line written as real copy for a real situation (an error, a price, a welcome) |
| **Messaging** | The few things the brand always says, and the proof behind each | Three or four pillars at most, each tied to a real product strength or evidence |
| **Visual direction** | What it should feel like, reference points, the owner's fixed choices (a colour, a typeface, a mark), and what it must never look like | Specific enough to steer and to reject a wrong direction, and loose enough to leave the system to the designer |
| **Assets** | Where the logo, icon, art, photography and existing guidelines live | Paths or links, with who owns them |

## From the owner

- What should people feel on first contact? After a month of use?
- Which brands, inside or outside the category, do they admire, and for what specifically?
- What would be embarrassing for this brand to look or sound like?
- Are any visual choices already fixed (existing logo, colour, typeface, illustration style)?
- Who is the audience's taste shaped by?
- Is the name settled? How attached are they to it?

Showing options helps here: sample lines of copy in two or three voices, or short descriptions of contrasting visual directions. Let the owner react. Reactions are easier and more reliable than specifications.

## Research and compute

- **Name checks:** check domain availability, handles, app-store names and confusing uses on the public web. Search accessible public trademark aggregators as preliminary evidence, recording their coverage and date; an aggregator is not official clearance. For official registries (e.g., USPTO, EUIPO, WIPO Global Brand Database and the target market’s national registry, such as CIPC), prepare an owner-run search: give the registry link, exact-name and similar-spelling/sounding queries, relevant goods/services and candidate Nice classes, filter choices and concrete steps. Classes guide the search; related goods or services can conflict across classes. Ask the owner to run the official searches and return the queries, filters and results. Verify that the intended query ran and results loaded before recording “no matches”; blocked automation, an empty page, a failed request or a login/CAPTCHA wall means unverified, never available. WIPO coverage does not replace the relevant national/regional registers. Keep official checks open until their evidence is returned, distinguish preliminary screening from clearance, and recommend professional clearance before significant spend on the name. Record all findings and limits with the date in `research/`.

  Search guidance: [USPTO comprehensive clearance searches](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks), [USPTO coordinated classes](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/using-coordinated-classes-your-federal-trademark-search), and [WIPO database coverage](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database/index).
- **Category visual and verbal landscape:** how competitors look and sound, so the brand can deliberately differ.
- **Audience language:** words the target users actually use (from `market.md` research), so the voice sounds like them rather than like marketing.

## Belongs / doesn't

| In | Out |
|---|---|
| Owner-fixed colours, typefaces and marks, as fixed choices | Token tables, type scales, spacing systems, component styles |
| Direction, references and red lines | Page layouts, UI mockups |
| Voice traits with sample lines | A full copy deck, website copy |
| Where assets live | The asset files themselves |

## Common weaknesses

- Personality traits every brand claims (friendly, innovative, trustworthy).
- Visual direction as a list of adjectives with no references and no "never".
- A name adopted without any availability check.
- Positioning in `brand.md` that contradicts `market.md`.
