# market.md: who buys and how the project wins them

Two halves: **research** (what the market is) and **strategy** (how the project enters and wins it). The research half should be the most evidence-heavy part of the blueprint. The strategy half turns that evidence into choices the rest of the blueprint depends on: the price in `business.md`, the users in `product.md`, the positioning in `brand.md`.

## Establishes

### Market research

| Section | Answers | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| **Segments** | The distinct groups who have the problem: who they are, where they are, how they buy, and who pays vs. who uses | Segments are defined by behaviour or need, not demographics alone. Buyer and user are separated where they differ |
| **Size** | How many potential customers and how much they could spend, per segment | Estimated bottom-up (count × price) and cross-checked top-down, with method, sources and dates. SAM and a realistic obtainable share are distinguished from total market |
| **Alternatives** | What people use today, including spreadsheets, manual work, hiring someone and doing nothing | Each has the reason people choose it and where it fails them |
| **Competitors** | The direct and adjacent products that matter: positioning, pricing, strengths, and weaknesses in their own customers' words | Built from competitors' own sites, pricing pages and customer reviews, dated. Limited to the competitors that change a decision |
| **Demand evidence** | Signals that people want this and will pay: search volume, community activity, waitlists, pre-sales, interviews, competitors' traction | Distinguishes observed behaviour (paid, signed up, searched) from stated interest |
| **Forces** | Trends, regulation, platform dependencies and seasonality that shape the market | Each names its effect on the project |

### Market strategy

| Section | Answers | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| **Beachhead** | The first segment to win and why that one: most pain, reachable, can pay, references others | One segment is chosen and the choice is argued. "Everyone" is not a beachhead |
| **Positioning** | For whom, what category, what it is best at, unlike which alternative, because of what | A sentence a competitor could not claim |
| **Value proposition** | Why the beachhead switches: the gain against the cost and risk of switching | It names the switching costs and how they are lowered |
| **Channels** | How the beachhead finds, tries and buys the product, and why those channels suit this segment and price | Each channel has a rationale, rough acquisition-cost expectations (feeding `business.md`), and a way to test it cheaply |
| **Expansion** | The sequence of segments or markets after the beachhead | The order is argued, not listed |
| **Defensibility** | What keeps winners winning: network effects, data, switching costs, brand, distribution, cost | Honest. "First mover" alone is not a moat |

## From the owner

- Who have they talked to? What did those people say and do?
- Which customers can they reach personally or through existing channels?
- Which competitors do they respect, and which do they think are beatable, and why?
- Where do they think the market is heading?
- Are there segments they want to avoid, for ethical, practical or personal reasons?

If the owner has not spoken to potential customers, say so plainly and suggest they do before the strategy is approved. Offer a short interview guide focused on past behaviour ("tell me about the last time you…"), not hypothetical interest. Record what they learn in `research/`.

## Research and compute

This document relies on `references/research.md`. In particular:
- Size bottom-up from counts you can source (businesses in a category, app users, professionals by occupation), multiplied by a realistic price, and show the arithmetic.
- Mine competitors' reviews (app stores, review sites, forums) for recurring complaints. They are the cheapest customer research there is.
- Record competitors' pricing with the date. It feeds `business.md`.

## Belongs / doesn't

| In | Out |
|---|---|
| Sourced, dated market facts and the method behind estimates | Unsourced numbers; analyst-report headlines without a method |
| The few competitors that change a decision, and why | Exhaustive feature-comparison matrices |
| The channel strategy and why it fits | Campaign plans, posting schedules, ad copy |
| Positioning as a claim | The brand expression of it (`brand.md`) |

## Common weaknesses

- A large top-down TAM standing in for the market the project can actually reach.
- Competitor analysis from memory instead of their current pages.
- "Content marketing and SEO" as the channel strategy for everything, with no fit to the segment.
- A beachhead that is really three segments.
