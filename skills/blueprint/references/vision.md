# vision.md: why this exists

The first document and the one every other document answers to. It holds the core idea, the future the project is working toward, the principles that settle trade-offs, and why this owner is placed to do it. If `vision.md` is vague, everything downstream will be generic, so spend the time here.

## Establishes

| Section | Answers | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| **Core idea** | The problem, who has it, what the project does about it, and why now: one paragraph, then the single sentence that captures it | The sentence is false for every competitor and would be understood by a target user |
| **Insight** | The non-obvious truth the project is built on: what the owner believes that most people in the space do not | It is a belief someone could reasonably disagree with, and the evidence for it is recorded |
| **Vision** | The world when this has won, concretely, at a stated horizon | A reader can picture it. It says what is different for users and for the market, not how big the company is |
| **Philosophy** | 3–7 principles that decide trade-offs when requirements conflict | Each names the tension it resolves and what it sacrifices ("local first: we accept slower sync to keep data on the device") |
| **Why us** | What makes this owner or team suited to it: access, expertise, assets, distribution, conviction | It is honest. Where there is no edge, it says what will stand in for one |

## From the owner

The owner usually holds this document in their head without having written it down. Your job is to draw it out and sharpen it. Useful threads (illustrative, follow whatever is productive):

- Where did the idea come from? What did they see or experience?
- Who have they watched struggle with the problem, and what did those people do about it?
- What would they build even if it never made money? What would they refuse to build even if it did?
- When two good things conflict (speed vs. privacy, simplicity vs. power, growth vs. margin), which wins?
- What does success look like in five or ten years, for users rather than for the company?
- Why are they the one to do this? What do they have that others do not?

Play the idea back in your own words early, and let the owner correct it. Their corrections usually reveal the insight.

## Research and compute

- **Evidence the problem is real and felt:** complaints, workarounds, forum threads, reviews of alternatives, search interest. The goal is to confirm the pain exists and to learn how people describe it, not to size the market yet (that is `market.md`).
- **Why now:** the recent shift that makes this possible or necessary (technology, regulation, cost curves, behaviour), with sources.
- **Prior attempts:** who tried this before and what happened. A graveyard of similar products is important evidence either way.

## Belongs / doesn't

| In | Out |
|---|---|
| The problem, in the user's terms | Feature lists or solution design |
| The belief the project rests on | Mission-statement filler ("empower", "revolutionise") |
| Principles that decide real trade-offs | Values any company would claim ("quality", "integrity") |
| An honest account of the owner's edge | Market sizing (`market.md`) or revenue goals (`business.md`) |

## Common weaknesses

- A core idea that describes a category, not a product ("an AI productivity tool").
- A vision that is a revenue target in disguise.
- Principles that never cost anything. If a principle has never forced a "no", it is a slogan.
- "Why now" with no actual change named.
